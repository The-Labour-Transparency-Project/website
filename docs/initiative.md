# The initiative

The Labour Transparency Project explores how agricultural and horticultural supply chains can make labour relationships visible, traceable and accountable.

## The problem is structural

Labour arrangements commonly span growers, employers, contractors, subcontractors, labour-hire providers and seasonal workers moving between sites. Many of these relationships are managed responsibly, but the records often sit in different organisations and systems.

When the system cannot quickly establish who employed a worker, who supplied the labour, where work happened and who held responsibility, investigations become slower and risk can spread well beyond the organisation involved.

## A different framing

The project does not begin with the question, “Who should govern labour compliance?” It begins with a prior question: “What information and relationships need to be visible for good governance to work?”

The answer is labour transparency infrastructure: durable, verifiable links among workers, employers, labour providers and production sites.

> This is the labour equivalent of product traceability.

## What this project is — and is not

| This project is | This project is not |
| --- | --- |
| A shared framework for discussing transparent labour supply chains | A public database of workers or employment records |
| A technology-neutral standard | A mandate for one technology vendor or central platform |
| A basis for more proportionate assurance | A replacement for employment law, audits or worker voice |
| A way to protect responsible participants | A mechanism for public surveillance |

## Intended outcomes

- Better protection for workers.
- Clearer accountability throughout contractor chains.
- Faster, more precise incident investigation and containment.
- Lower unnecessary compliance burden for demonstrably responsible operators.
- Stronger confidence in New Zealand agricultural exports.
