# The Labour Transparency Framework

The framework has four connected layers. Each has a distinct role; none substitutes for the others.

<div class="diagram">
  <div><strong>1. Labour Transparency Code</strong>Defines the outcomes that participating supply chains expect to see.</div>
  <div><strong>2. Labour Identity Infrastructure</strong>Creates verifiable links between workers, employers, labour providers and worksites.</div>
  <div><strong>3. Assurance and governance</strong>Uses those records for risk-based assurance, investigation and improvement.</div>
  <div><strong>4. Market adoption</strong>Embeds expectations through industry programmes, buyer requirements, certification or export systems.</div>
</div>

## 1. The Code

The Code sets minimum transparency expectations. It defines **what** should be capable of being demonstrated, without prescribing a specific technical solution.

## 2. Labour identity infrastructure

Sometimes described as Know Your Workforce (KYW), this is the ability to verify and connect:

- a worker to their responsible employer;
- an employer to declared labour providers;
- labour participation to a worksite and period; and
- a production site to the relevant supply chain.

Interoperability matters. Useful records may be held in payroll, onboarding, time-and-attendance, contractor-management or onsite systems. The aim is reliable exchange and verification, not forced centralisation.

## 3. Assurance and governance

Audits, worker reporting, investigation and risk monitoring remain essential. They become more effective when they draw on continuous, attributable records rather than relying only on point-in-time documentation.

## 4. Market adoption

Standards acquire practical force when supply chains adopt them. Adoption could occur through assurance programmes, certification schemes, buyer requirements or export supply-chain expectations.
