# Containment Is the Practical Value of Visibility

<ArticleInfographic src="/11-containment-through-visibility-infographic.png" alt="Infographic 11: Containment Through Visibility" />

## Traceability does not eliminate risk. It narrows and contains it.

When workforce relationships are opaque, an incident can create uncertainty far beyond the original problem. Investigators may not know which workers, contractors, sites or outputs are affected, when the relevant arrangement operated or which entity held a particular responsibility.

The response may then become broad by default: wide investigations, suspended relationships, product withdrawals or market concern.

Where structured workforce traceability exists, organisations can ask more precise questions:

- which entity engaged the worker?
- which site was involved?
- which contractor or subcontractor was responsible?
- which records confirm the relationship?
- which other actors or outputs are affected?

The response can then follow a practical sequence: **identify** the concern and relevant relationship; **isolate** the affected cohort and any immediate risk; **investigate** with the necessary evidence and due process; and **remedy** harm while communicating clearly with affected people. A mapped system does not decide the outcome in advance, but it makes the scope of responsible action easier to establish.

Containment must be worker-centred. It should protect people from further harm, preserve fair process, avoid blame by association and provide meaningful routes to support and remedy. It is not a promise that incidents will be small or that risk has been eliminated. It is a governance capability that prevents uncertainty from becoming system-wide by default.

> Visibility turns systemic uncertainty into defined, actionable risk.
