# Participation Needs Incentives

<ArticleInfographic src="/12-participation-needs-incentives-infographic.png" alt="Infographic 12: Participation Needs Incentives" />

## Transparency becomes systemic when participation creates practical value.

No assurance infrastructure succeeds through obligation alone. Independent participants need reasons to participate and a clear understanding of what they gain in return for contributing trusted signals. Moral pressure or data extraction alone will not create durable, voluntary participation.

Potential benefits include:

- reduced duplicate administration;
- simpler onboarding;
- better integration with payroll and time systems;
- clearer contracting relationships;
- faster dispute resolution;
- stronger evidence for responsible practice;
- more proportionate responses when incidents occur.

Industry conveners also matter. Exporters, regulators, sector bodies and major buyers can help establish shared expectations and create incentives for participation without turning transparency into a purely top-down exercise.

The value differs by role. Workers need voice, correction and safe pathways to challenge harmful records or decisions. Employers, contractors and labour providers can benefit from simpler onboarding, less duplicated administration and clearer relationships. Buyers and industry bodies can make better assurance decisions; auditors and assurance providers can work with more reliable context; regulators and public institutions can gain a clearer basis for proportionate action.

Participation can become a reinforcing cycle: participants contribute relevant information, authorised parties verify it, the system is used for legitimate decisions, lessons are learned, practices improve and wider participation becomes worthwhile. This only works when the guardrails are equally clear: privacy, proportional access, transparent purpose and remedy must accompany every claimed benefit.

The first New Zealand agriculture and horticulture trial can test whether transparency improves the way work is organised, rather than simply adding another reporting burden. Industry conveners can help establish shared expectations, but the model must remain respectful of independent roles and must not imply participation is compulsory.

> Adoption follows value. A trusted system must help participants do better work.
