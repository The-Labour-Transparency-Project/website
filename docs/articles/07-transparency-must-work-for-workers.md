# Transparency Must Work for Workers

<ArticleInfographic src="/07-transparency-must-work-for-workers-infographic.png" alt="Infographic 07: Transparency Must Work for Workers" />

## Workers should be participants in transparency systems, not merely sources of data.

A system can improve institutional visibility while still failing workers. Labour transparency must therefore include agency, voice and remedy.

Worker agency should be present before, during and after a workforce relationship. Before work begins, people should be able to understand clear terms and how their information will be used. During the relationship, they need safe ways to provide feedback and exercise meaningful control. If a concern, decision or record causes harm, they need routes to challenge it and seek remedy.

That includes:

- clear employment terms;
- informed consent and meaningful control;
- safe feedback channels;
- grievance and remedy pathways;
- the ability to challenge or correct records;
- collective representation;
- protection against retaliation and surveillance.

Worker voice is not an optional extra. It helps reveal conditions that formal records may not show and gives workers a role in shaping how trust is understood. It must sit alongside—not replace—collective representation, independent support and fair remedy.

## A rights-based test

Any workforce transparency system should ask:

1. What does the worker know?
2. What can the worker control?
3. How can the worker challenge an error?
4. Who can access the information?
5. What remedy exists when harm occurs?

There is a meaningful difference between visibility *about* workers and transparency *with* workers. The first may make institutions better informed while leaving the person represented unable to see, influence or correct the system. The second treats workers as participants and sources of assurance, not merely data subjects or evidence containers.

For this reason, a digital system alone is never the answer. Its governance must be understandable, its access boundaries clear and its feedback pathways safe from retaliation. A system that cannot be questioned by the people it affects cannot claim to provide complete assurance.

> Transparency is not successful if institutions become more visible while workers remain unheard.
