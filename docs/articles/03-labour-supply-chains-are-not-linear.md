# Labour Supply Chains Are Not Linear

<ArticleInfographic src="/03-labour-supply-chains-are-not-linear-infographic.png" alt="Infographic 03: Labour Supply Chains Are Not Linear" />

## Workforce relationships form networks, not simple chains.

Products often move through a sequence of defined stages: inputs, production, processing and distribution. Labour may move through overlapping arrangements involving producers, employers, contractors, subcontractors, labour providers, production sites and workers. Oversight bodies and systems of record add further relationships without necessarily sitting in the same organisation.

Each relationship can carry different responsibilities, records and expectations. A worker may be recruited through one organisation, deployed to another site and paid or supported through several systems. The arrangement can also change over a season or between jobs. A straight-line model cannot reliably represent that reality.

## The three visibility gaps

### Relationship visibility

Who is contractually or operationally connected to whom?

### Worker attribution

Which legal entity, employer, contractor or production site is each worker linked to?

### Event traceability

If a concern or breach occurs, can it be isolated to a defined set of relationships or outputs?

When those connections are not structured, answers depend on reconstruction. People must match documents, contacts and memories across organisations. That work is slow, can become adversarial and is often incomplete precisely when a timely response is needed.

## Why the network matters

The network is not a problem to be simplified away. It is the reality that assurance must represent. The design response is to map meaningful relationships, record meaningful events, preserve authority and make responsibility legible. This does not mean every participant sees every record or that a central body owns them all.

A useful system instead makes authorised relationships and references visible while allowing each participant to retain appropriate control of its own source records. It gives a decision-maker enough context to ask: who is connected, which arrangement applies and what must happen next? That is a more honest foundation for governance than pretending the workforce system is a single chain.

> The architecture of labour is networked, even when the records are not.
