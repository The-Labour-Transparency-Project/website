# The ten rules

## 1. Every worker is linked to a responsible employer

Labour supply chains should clearly identify the legally accountable employer for every worker.

## 2. Every labour provider is declared

Contractors, subcontractors and labour-hire providers should be visible within the supply chain.

## 3. Employment agreements are verifiable

Employment or engagement terms should be documented and available for verification.

## 4. Identity and right to work are verified

Employers should confirm that workers are legally entitled to undertake the work they perform.

## 5. Work is attributable to specific sites

It should be possible to establish where work was performed within the production system.

## 6. Labour participation is traceable

Systems should enable verification of who worked, where they worked and under which employer.

## 7. Payroll records reflect work performed

Hours worked and wages paid should be capable of verification through payroll and time records.

## 8. Undeclared subcontracting is not acceptable

Labour supply arrangements should be transparent and visible to relevant supply-chain participants.

## 9. Labour records are maintained in verifiable systems

Records should support assurance and investigation when required.

## 10. Supply chains share responsibility for transparency

Organisations should ensure labour providers within their supply chains meet the transparency expectations of the Code.
