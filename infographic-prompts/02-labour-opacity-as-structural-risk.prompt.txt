Use case: editorial infographic.

Create a one-page vertical infographic for The Labour Transparency Project titled:
“Labour Opacity Is a Structural Risk.”

Subtitle: “When workforce relationships are unclear, risk cannot be understood or contained.”

Audience: sponsors, industry participants, regulators, workers’ representatives and researchers. Include examples relevant to New Zealand agriculture and horticulture without limiting the concept to one sector.

Format and style:
- 1080 × 1350 px, 4:5 vertical layout.
- Warm ivory background with calm institutional editorial design.
- Flat vector-like geometry, restrained linework, generous whitespace and high mobile readability.
- Palette: deep navy #102A43, forest green #1F5B52, muted ochre #B58A3B, neutral grey #9A9E9A, with red used only sparingly for a warning state.
- No logos, product names, trademarks, partner names or invented statistics.

Core message: labour opacity is not only an ethical concern. It creates regulatory exposure, reputational damage and operational uncertainty because organisations cannot reliably identify relationships, affected workers or the boundaries of an incident.

Visual structure:
1. A central “opacity” area showing incomplete or disconnected records between a production site, employer, contractor, labour provider and worker.
2. Three risk columns: “Regulatory”, “Reputational” and “Operational”. Use short phrases: changing obligations, loss of confidence, slow investigation, broad exposure, unclear accountability.
3. A before-and-after panel: “Fragmented visibility” versus “Defined relationships”. In the second state, show a bounded cohort and a clear route from incident to responsible actors.
4. A practical conclusion: “Visibility does not eliminate risk. It makes risk identifiable, attributable and containable.”

Avoid disaster imagery, sensationalism, literal chains, a shield, a generic compliance tick or surveillance imagery. The design should feel analytical and constructive rather than accusatory.

Footer line: “The problem is often architectural: what the system cannot see, it cannot govern.”

Ensure all text is exact, legible and spelled correctly.
