Use case: editorial infographic.

Create a one-page vertical infographic for The Labour Transparency Project titled:
“Transparency Must Work for Workers.”

Subtitle: “Not just reporting on workers — building voice, agency and remedy into assurance.”

Audience: sponsors, industry participants, regulators, workers’ representatives and researchers. Include a subtle New Zealand context without using national symbols or reducing the issue to one sector.

Format and style:
- 1080 × 1350 px, 4:5 vertical layout.
- Warm ivory background; humane, calm and institutionally credible editorial style.
- Flat vector-like geometry, simple neutral participant symbols rather than portraits, generous whitespace and high mobile readability.
- Palette: deep navy #102A43, forest green #1F5B52, muted ochre #B58A3B, neutral grey #9A9E9A.
- No logos, trademarks, partner names or invented statistics.

Core message: labour transparency is legitimate only when workers can understand, influence and challenge the records and decisions that affect them.

Visual structure:
1. A central circle labelled “Worker agency”, surrounded by six connected principles: “Clear terms”, “Consent and control”, “Safe feedback”, “Grievance and remedy”, “Representation”, “Ability to correct records”.
2. A comparison panel: “Visibility about workers” versus “Transparency with workers”.
3. A lifecycle showing how worker voice can enter before, during and after a workforce relationship.
4. A concluding panel: workers are participants and sources of assurance, not merely data subjects or evidence containers.

Make the design respectful and non-paternalistic. Avoid worker stereotypes, surveillance cameras, literal shields and generic compliance imagery. Do not imply that a digital system replaces representation, collective voice or remedy.

Footer line: “Transparency is stronger when the people represented in the system can be heard within it.”

Ensure all text is exact, legible and spelled correctly.
